# Zarr at Scale: Reads and Writes from 10 MB to 10 GB

<ZarrNav current="sizeladder" />

A notebook that shows what reading and writing Zarr costs as a store grows from 10 MB to 10 GB. It follows two workloads:

- **County wind:** a model that reads a small grid for a long time. The grid is 0.01 degrees (about 1 km) over a one degree box, hourly.
- **Global 11 km:** a model that reads the global 0.1 degree grid, hourly.

Both stores have the variables of the [weather features notebook](zarr-weather-features): `t2m`, `u10`, `v10` and `msl`. The notebook makes the store itself, with `write_era5_cube`. The `RUNG` setting picks its size.

It takes the store through three steps:

1. **Read** the store five ways, each timed. They are the full table, a box over all time, 100 sites, wind speed with an hourly area mean, and one hour as a tile. Functions: `gbx_zarr_read` and `gbx_zarr_sample`.
2. **Write a table to Zarr.** Read the cells, add `wind_speed` in Spark columns, and write them as a plain store and as a managed store: `gbx_zarr_write`.
3. **Append one more day** to both stores, read both back, and check that their sums equal the source.

## Settings

The first cell holds the settings. Change them to run another size or shape.

| Setting | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `CATALOG`, `SCHEMA` | a schema you can write to | Holds the managed store's manifest table. |
| `WORK_DIR` | a Volume folder | Holds the input store and each run's output. |
| `FAMILY` | `county_wind`, `global_11km` | The shape of the store. |
| `RUNG` | `10MB`, `50MB`, `100MB`, `500MB`, `1GB`, `2GB`, `5GB`, `10GB` | The uncompressed size of the four variables. The store grows along time. |
| `GENERATE` | `True`, `False` | `True` makes the store when it is missing and reuses it when it is there. `False` expects a store that exists. |
| `CLEAN_UP`, `DROP_STORE` | `True`, `False` | `CLEAN_UP` drops this run's table and folder. `DROP_STORE` also deletes the input store. |

Every run gets its own `RUN_ID`, so its table and folder never collide with another run's.

---

## Run order

1. **Install and restart.** The `%pip install` cell is followed by `%restart_python`.
2. **Settings.** Set the schema, the Volume, the family and the rung. A second cell names this run's table and folders.
3. **The shape of the store.** `ladder_shape` turns the family and the rung into a number of hours and a window of the grid.
4. **Make or reuse the store.** `write_era5_cube` builds the store one shard at a time. The notebook then reads its labels with xarray.
5. **Reads.** Each read prints one line: its wall-clock time and what it returned.
6. **Table to Zarr.** The notebook writes the cells as a plain store and as a managed store, and reads both back.
7. **Append a day.** The notebook adds 24 hours to both stores and checks them against the source plus the new day. A one-hour store skips this step.
8. **Timings, reference timings and clean up.**

## Data flow

```text
write_era5_cube(hours, step, box, chunks, shards)       Zarr v3, sharded, zstd, float32
        │
        ▼  <WORK_DIR>/<family>/<rung>.zarr
        ├─▶ gbx_zarr_read(output="table")          full table
        ├─▶ gbx_zarr_read(sel=area)                 a box over all time
        ├─▶ gbx_zarr_sample(100 sites)              the hourly series at the sites
        ├─▶ gbx_zarr_read(u10, v10) + Spark         wind speed and the hourly area mean
        ├─▶ gbx_zarr_read(output="tiles")           one hour as a tile
        │
        ▼  cells + wind_speed (Spark columns)
           gbx_zarr_write(dim_cols, coords, complete=True, transactional=False)   plain store
           gbx_zarr_write(dim_cols, coords, complete=True)                        managed store
        │
        ▼  gbx_zarr_write(mode="append", append_dim="time")   one more day, both stores
Read both back: same cell count and sums as the source
```

## Plain or managed

A **plain** store (`transactional=False`) is a Zarr v3 store at a path. It has no manifest table, no versions and no commit. Other tools read it as it is. Use it for one writer at a time.

A **managed** store (the default) adds a manifest table. It gives all-or-nothing writes, versions, time travel and safe concurrent writers. Use it when more than one job writes, or when you need an earlier version.

See [Plain stores](../writers/zarr#plain-stores-opt-in) and [Write from a table of cells](../writers/zarr#write-from-a-table-of-cells).

## Reference timings

These timings were measured on two kinds of compute. **Your numbers will differ.** Compute, the Volume, and the
other work on the cluster all change them.

- **DBR 19:** Databricks Runtime 19, two workers of four cores each (`m6gd.xlarge`), dedicated access.
- **Serverless:** Serverless environment 6. Its compute varies from run to run, so use these numbers to see the
  shape of the cost, not to compare with a fixed cluster.

Each time is the median of three runs, in seconds, for the steps in this notebook. The first run is cold. The
global stores below 500 MB are one hour of a regional window, so "100 MB" global is a different shape from "1 GB".

**County wind** (0.01 degree grid over a one degree box; 24 hours in a chunk)

| Store | Compute | Full table | Box | 100 sites | Wind speed | One-hour tile | Write plain | Write managed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 MB | DBR 19 | 2.9 s | 2.5 s | 2.7 s | 2.3 s | 2.7 s | 9.8 s | 23.6 s |
| 100 MB | Serverless | 3.2 s | 3.0 s | 3.3 s | 2.9 s | 2.2 s | 10.6 s | 16.0 s |
| 1 GB | DBR 19 | 13.1 s | 9.7 s | 7.1 s | 6.9 s | 3.1 s | 37.8 s | 40.6 s |
| 1 GB | Serverless | 14.9 s | 13.7 s | 10.0 s | 9.5 s | 2.3 s | 41.7 s | 45.8 s |
| 10 GB | DBR 19 | 113.3 s | 86.0 s | 53.0 s | 53.7 s | 3.3 s | 333.6 s | 307.8 s |
| 10 GB | Serverless | 55.0 s | 42.6 s | 18.7 s | 22.7 s | 3.2 s | 112.3 s | 149.6 s |

**Global 11 km** (0.1 degree grid)

| Store | Compute | Full table | Box | 100 sites | Wind speed | One-hour tile | Write plain | Write managed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 MB | DBR 19 | 12.8 s | 1.5 s | 1.2 s | 1.3 s | 4.6 s | 21.2 s | 21.4 s |
| 100 MB | Serverless | 18.3 s | 2.2 s | 1.7 s | 2.0 s | 5.3 s | 32.0 s | 38.3 s |
| 1 GB | DBR 19 | 23.8 s | 2.7 s | 1.4 s | 1.7 s | 4.4 s | 45.2 s | 48.5 s |
| 1 GB | Serverless | 13.2 s | 5.8 s | 2.0 s | 2.1 s | 6.8 s | 22.0 s | 30.6 s |
| 10 GB | DBR 19 | 151.3 s | 2.4 s | 3.0 s | 2.0 s | 10.0 s | 284.6 s | 297.4 s |
| 10 GB | Serverless | 54.6 s | 2.9 s | 2.9 s | 2.5 s | 7.8 s | 94.8 s | 71.9 s |

What the numbers show:

- **A full-table read grows with the size of the store.** At 10 GB it takes 113 s (county) and 151 s (global) on DBR 19, and about 55 s on Serverless.
- **Reads of a part of the store stay short.** On the global grid the box, the 100 sites, the wind speed and the one-hour tile take 10 s or less at every rung. They read only the chunks they need.
- **A county store has one spatial chunk.** Its box, site and wind-speed reads therefore grow with the length of the series.
- **Plain and managed writes are close from 1 GB.** At 100 MB the plain write is faster, by a fixed cost of seconds that the manifest and the commit add.

## Gotchas

- **A warm-up read comes first.** The first read of a session starts the Zarr readers on the cluster. The notebook does it before the timings, so they leave out the start-up.
- **Pass `coords` and `complete=True`.** Without them the writer scans the table once before it writes. With them the table is read once. See [Zarr to Zarr: compute on cells](../writers/zarr#zarr-to-zarr-compute-on-cells).
- **Small global rungs are one hour.** Below 500 MB the global store is one hour of a window of the grid. It has no day to append.
- **The data is synthetic.** The values are smooth made-up fields with noise. They compress like real weather data, so read the timings as the shape of the cost, not as a forecast of yours.
